## LinkedIn post

August in Peoria means the heat doesn't ask — it tells you. As a family medicine physician in the West Valley, I see the same pattern every summer: patients who work outdoors, parents trying to keep their kids active, and folks who just want to enjoy a normal afternoon without ending up in urgent care. The ones who get through it best aren't the ones who tough it out — they're the ones who pay attention early and plan a little.

My new article covers the practical things that actually make a difference: who needs extra care (older adults, patients on certain medications, outdoor workers including the TSMC and tech workforce in our area), the early warning signs of heat illness that are easy to brush off, and a short list of habits that fit into a real West Valley day — start hydrated, drink before you're thirsty, pair water with food, and take breaks in real shade during the hottest hours.
