Summer Heat Safety: What It Means for You and Your Family in the West Valley

Practical heat-safety habits for West Valley families, outdoor workers, and anyone facing another August in the Arizona sun.

Peoria, Arizona · August 2026

Why August Feels Different

Simple Habits That Make the Day Easier

  • **Start the day hydrated.** Drink water before you feel thirsty. If you're heading out to work or exercise, have a glass or two in the hour before. Thirst is a delayed signal — by the time you feel it, you're already mildly dehydrated.

  • **Pair water with food.** Salty snacks, fruit, and regular meals help your body hold onto fluids. Plain water alone doesn't always cut it on long hot days, especially if you're sweating heavily. A normal balanced meal with some electrolytes is often better than an expensive sports drink.

  • **Schedule around the heat.** If your job or routine puts you outside, move the hardest work to early morning or evening when possible. The hours between roughly 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. are the most demanding on your body. That's not always avoidable — I know a lot of folks in construction, landscaping, and warehouse work can't just shift their schedule — but even a short break in real shade during the hottest stretch helps.

  • **Take shade breaks seriously.** A break in air conditioning is ideal, but real shade — under a tree, a covered porch, a tent — is better than direct sun. A five-minute break every hour is a small investment that pays off.

  • **Dress for it.** Lightweight, light-colored clothing and a wide-brimmed hat aren't style statements — they're heat strategy. Sunscreen matters too, but it's the clothing and shade that do the heavy lifting.

  • **Check on the people around you.** Older neighbors, family members with chronic conditions, and anyone who lives alone are more vulnerable to heat. A quick check-in call or visit during an extreme-heat day can make a real difference. Heat illness often looks subtle at first — fatigue, headache, not thinking clearly — and people who are isolated may not have anyone noticing.

When Heat Goes Too Far

When to Call the Doctor

  • Symptoms that don't improve after an hour or two of cooling down and drinking water.
  • Repeated heat exhaustion — if you're getting knocked down by heat multiple times in a season, there may be something else going on worth checking.
  • New or worsening symptoms in someone with a chronic condition during hot weather.
  • Any sign of confusion, disorientation, or stopped sweating in a hot environment — that's an emergency, not a wait-and-see situation.
