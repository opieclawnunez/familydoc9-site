The 2 A.M. Bathroom Trip Is Not a Personality Trait.

If you're a man over 50 and your bladder wakes you up more than your alarm does, that's not just getting older. An enlarged prostate is one common cause - and whatever the cause, it's worth getting checked, because there's plenty we can do about it.

2026-11-04By Dr. Manuel Nunez

Here's a scene I hear about all the time. A guy in his fifties tells me, almost embarrassed: "Doc, I get up three times a night to pee." Then he says the part that gets me: "I figured it's just what happens now." No. That's not a birthday thing. That's a plumbing thing - and plumbing can be fixed.

November is Movember - the mustache month for men's health - so let's talk about the gland nobody wants to talk about.

What's actually going on down there

Your prostate sits right under the bladder and wraps around the urethra - the tube urine travels through. As it grows with age, and it usually does, it squeezes that tube like a thumb over a garden hose. Result: a weak or stop-and-start stream, trouble getting going, dribbling at the end, the feeling you didn't quite finish, and those nightly trips to the bathroom.

This is incredibly common. An enlarged prostate is the most common prostate problem in men over 50. About 5 to 6 percent of men ages 40 to 64 deal with it, and roughly a third of men 65 and older. If this is you, you are in a lot of company.

Three things I want every man to hear

  1. An enlarged prostate is not cancer, and it does not raise your risk of prostate cancer. The official name is benign prostatic hyperplasia - the word benign is right there in the name.
  2. The size of the prostate does not match the misery. A big one can be quiet, and a slightly enlarged one can make your life miserable. What matters is how much your symptoms bother you.
  3. These symptoms can also come from other things: a urinary tract infection, an inflamed prostate, bladder problems, and yes, sometimes prostate cancer. That's why you get them checked instead of guessing. The check itself is easy - a conversation, an exam, sometimes simple tests. If your doctor brings up a PSA blood test, that's a decision you make together, not an automatic order.

Some symptoms mean go now

If you cannot urinate at all, that is an emergency - get seen right away. Also get seen right away for: painful, frequent, urgent urination along with fever and chills; blood in your urine; or serious pain low in your belly or around the urinary tract. Don't tough those out.

What you can do tonight

A lot of this gets meaningfully better with a few habits:

  • Ease off fluids a couple of hours before bed - and before long car rides or meetings.
  • Cut back on alcohol and caffeine, especially after dinner. Both irritate the bladder and make you go more.
  • Don't hold it for ages. Go when you need to go.
  • Take your time and empty fully. Some men do better with double voiding: go, wait a moment, then try once more.
  • Stay physically active - it may lower your risk of a bothersome prostate and it helps the symptoms too.
  • Check your medicine cabinet. Common cold and allergy medicines - decongestants and antihistamines - can make all of this worse. Ask before you grab the usual stuff.

And if that's not enough, we've got more

Medications work well for a lot of men. One group relaxes the muscles around the prostate and bladder neck - most men notice a difference within days. Another group slowly shrinks the prostate over three to six months; it can affect sex drive or performance for some men, and that's a conversation we have honestly before starting. Sometimes the combination works better than either one alone.

About the supplement shelf, saw palmetto and friends: the evidence is mixed. If you're taking something and feel it helps, tell me - just don't quietly hope instead of bringing it up.

And if pills don't cut it, urologists have real procedures now: steam therapy, small implants that lift the prostate off the tube, and the classic operation with the longest track record. Most men never need any of them. It's just good to know they exist.

The bottom line

You don't have to plan your life around bathrooms, map every rest stop on the drive, or accept three wake-ups a night as your new normal. Bring it up. It's one of the most fixable problems I see - and Movember is as good an excuse as any to finally say it out loud.
